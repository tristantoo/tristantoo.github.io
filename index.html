About

I build statistical models that have to hold up when someone acts on them: insurance pricing, healthcare claims, and forecasting. My work tests models on later data than they were trained on, checks that predicted probabilities match what happens, and reports when a model fails a check.

I am the first in my family to go to college. I finished a B.S. in Mathematical Sciences with a statistics concentration at VCU in May 2026 and started graduate study in Systems Modeling and Analysis that August. I am available for full-time analyst and statistician roles.

As a graduate teaching assistant I plan and teach full class sessions of Contemporary Mathematics for 85 students, write and grade their assessments, and keep the gradebook. Before that I spent nearly six years as a shift lead at Best Buy, leading about 30 people on a shift. Both jobs taught me the same thing: an answer only counts if the person in front of you can use it.

My statistics toolkit is GLMs (Poisson, binomial, Gamma, negative binomial), time series, Bayesian hierarchical models, the bootstrap, and calibration. I work in R, Python, SQL Server, Power BI, and SAS.

Research interests

Semiparametric and causal inference with time structure

Double machine learning, cross-fitting, and identification when data arrive over time.

Uncertainty quantification that survives shift

Conformal prediction and anytime-valid, sequential inference.

Bayesian and hierarchical modeling

Partial pooling, model diagnostics, and robustness.

Why learned advantages decay in adaptive competition

Drift, crowding, and adaptation in settings like game metas and markets.

Applications I have worked on include public health, time-series forecasting, data validation, and competitive esports.
